Japanese Cuisine

Onigiri: Rice balls wrapped in seaweed, usually with filling inside.

Onigiri - Rice Balls

First Discovered: Yayoi Period (300BC-250AD)

On November 12th 1987, archaeologists dug up carbonized lumps of rice grains from a building which dated back to the Yayoi Period in the Ishikawa Prefecture. These lumps were thought to be the oldest rice balls currently discovered, and interestingly, they were found to have traces that they were formed by hand.
Originally, sticky rice was wrapped in bamboo leaves and closely resembled another dish called Chimaki. It was during the Muromachi Period (1337-1573) that Nori, seaweed in Japanese, started to be used as wrapping and turned into the Onigiri we know today.

Did you know that the 'nigiri' in Onigiri means "to squeeze"?

Onigiri comes in many shapes and sizes, but the most common shapes used are triangular or formed into round balls. Triangular rice balls are actually inspired by mountains and is meant to receive the power of god.
Onigiri can have many fillings. The popular fillings you might have seen from TV shows or in-person might include: Umeboshi (pickled plum), Salmon and Tuna Mayo. Along with that, more fillings exist, which include: pickled vegetables, many meats like pork, and seafoods, such as mackerel, fish roe and squid.

Ramen: Boiled noodles served with broth, and toppings like meat and vegetables.

Ramen

First Discovered: Brought into Japan in 1859

Ramen was actually brought in by Chinese immigrants into the city of Yokohama back in the year 1859! The first dish similar to ramen was first sold in Northern Hokkaido in 1884 at a shop named Yowaken, in Hakodate city. This ramen dish was a shio, or salt-based, taste of ramen, and quickly spread across Japan by the Chinese community.
It was not until 1910 where a shop in the Asakusa district called Rairaiken was opened by Kanichi Ozaki and started serving shoyu (soy sauce) ramen, which started the rise of the ramen we know today. Unfortunately, the original shop closed forever after 1976, but the Shin-Yokohama Ramen Museum managed to recreate the original flavours of the shop in 2020, with the help of the original owner's grandson and great-grandson.

The most popular flavours of ramen include: Miso, Shoyu and Tonkotsu.
Tonkotsu Ramen was invented in Kurume, Fukuoka Prefecture, in 1937 by street vendor Tokio Miyamoto after he boiled pork bones for many hours and served it as a broth with his ramen.
Miso Ramen was invented in Sapporo, Hokkaido, in 1953 after the ramen shop Aji No Sanpei started offering ramen with miso included; this flavour of ramen was inspired after a customer requested his noodles to be included in his miso soup.

Curry Rice: Steamed rice served with curry roux sauce. Popular ingredients incorporated into the roux include potatoes, carrots, and beef cubes.

Japanese Curry Rice

First Discovered: 1872, during the Meiji Period (1868-1912)

The British navy started using curry from India to feed their large amount of sailors during their peak power of ruling. They ate curry with bread and modified Indian curry by adding vegetables and meat, making the curry thicker.
It is rumoured that the origins of Japanese curry rice came from a group of shipwrecked British sailors that were rescued off the Japanese coast, washing up along with their food rations, which included curry powder.
From there, the Japanese adapted the dish to use rice and a French-style roux that had a mixture of flour and fat as a thickener, resulting in a stew-like curry that was more creamy and sweet. Interestingly, Japanese curry resembles much closer to the British curry flavours than original Indian curry flavours, which was more spicy and aromatic.
During a rice shortage in 1876, William S. Clark, a U.S. agricultrualist that established Hokkaido University, suggested adding potatoes to curry rice and has been a popular edition ever since, even after the shortage has ended.

Curry rice also has different variants that consist of the roux: Curry Udon and Curry bread are some examples of foods that came after the invention of Japanese Curry Rice!

Sushi

First Discovered: Modern Sushi in 1820s, Ancestor of Sushi in Nara Period (8th century)

The oldest form of sushi was first invented in Southeast Asia during the 8th century. This form of sushi, called Narezushi, consisted of heavily fermented, gutted and descaled fish being preserved with salt and rice. The rice-wrapped fish was preserved with lactic acid bacteria, and the rice was thrown away and not eaten.
As time progressed, Narezushi evolved to use partially-fermented fish and for the rice to be consumed as well. To shorten the time required for fermentation, people started using Koji mold (Aspergillus oryzae), and others used vinegar or sake. During the 18th century however, vinegar started to become the main component to create the sour, fermented taste for the fish. And, by the 19th century, it was found that by mixing the rice in vinegar, it made the rice acidic and took less time to create the right flavour than fermenting the fish.

This was the beginning of the prototype of Nigiri-zushi that is arguably the most popular Japanese food today.
Today, many versions of sushi exist.
Some versions of sushi include: Nori-maki (seaweed-wrapped sushi) and Inari (sushi rice wrapped in fried tofu)
